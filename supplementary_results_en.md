# Supplementary Results 1. Division rate under nine treatments and two oxygen tensions (post hoc)

Division rate was measured in the Cellular Lifespan Study^13,14^ rather than manipulated by us, but nine of its treatment arms manipulate it in the same cell lines. Each treated culture was compared with the control culture of the same cell line and oxygen tension nearest in time in culture (224 of 224 treated cultures matched; median difference in days grown 0). The splicing score is the 177-gene score of Fig. 2. The change a treatment makes to the score follows the change it makes to the measured division rate (ρ = 0.49, *P* = 4 × 10⁻¹⁵, n = 224 cultures) and follows the change in the transcriptional proliferation score more closely (ρ = 0.79, *P* = 1 × 10⁻⁴⁹; Supplementary Fig. S1a, b). Contact inhibition, the largest manipulation of rate available, lowers the rate by 0.35 divisions per day and the score by 0.73 standard deviations (n = 41 cultures).

Pharmacology separates the two measures of proliferation. With the measured division rate held constant, five of the nine arms keep a residual effect on the splicing score at FDR < 0.05: dexamethasone +0.53 (95% CI 0.43 to 0.64), oligomycin +0.56 (0.41 to 0.71), mitochondrial nutrient-uptake inhibitors with dexamethasone +0.45 (0.31 to 0.58), oligomycin with dexamethasone +0.41 (0.16 to 0.66) and contact inhibition −0.17 (−0.31 to −0.03); a sixth, galactose, is nominal (+0.15, 95% CI 0.002 to 0.31, *P* = 0.048, FDR = 0.07). With the counted rate held constant the four drug treatments among them also carry a higher proliferation score than the rate predicts (dexamethasone +1.27, 1.10 to 1.44; mitochondrial nutrient-uptake inhibitors with dexamethasone +1.08; oligomycin with dexamethasone +0.98; oligomycin +0.54). In the matched differences the proliferation score rises under dexamethasone (+0.81) and under the inhibitors with dexamethasone (+0.28) and falls under oligomycin (−0.14) and oligomycin with dexamethasone (−0.27). With the proliferation score held constant instead, seven of the nine arms have no residual effect (Supplementary Fig. S1c, Supplementary Table 2); the exceptions are oligomycin, which raises the splicing score above what the proliferation score predicts (+0.28, 0.17 to 0.40), and 2-deoxyglucose, which lowers it (−0.32, −0.43 to −0.20). The proliferation score and the splicing score are strongly correlated with each other, so this second adjustment cannot separate them; what it shows is that these treatments do not move one without the other.

Oxygen tension leaves a further residual. The treatment comparison holds oxygen fixed by design, so it was examined separately among control cultures: fifteen cultures of three cell lines grown at 3% oxygen were matched to control cultures of the same line at 21% oxygen nearest in days grown (median difference 2.3 days). Growth at 3% oxygen lowers the measured division rate by 0.10 divisions per day (12 of 15 cultures lower) and the splicing score by 0.51 (12 of 15 lower), while the proliferation score does not move detectably (−0.26, 10 of 15 lower). In a model of the 104 control libraries with cell line and days grown as covariates, the splicing score is 0.21 lower at 3% oxygen with the measured rate held constant (95% CI −0.39 to −0.03, *P* = 0.022) and 0.33 lower with the proliferation score held constant (−0.44 to −0.23, *P* = 2 × 10⁻⁸). This residual is explained by neither measure of proliferation. It rests on three cell lines, and it counts against a reading in which proliferation is the only determinant of the score.

Apart from contact inhibition and the six cultures given oligomycin with dexamethasone (both −0.35 divisions per day), the arms move the division rate by 0.14 divisions per day or less, so this analysis tests the coupling under perturbation rather than across the full range of division rates. Treatments were not randomised to cell lines and the cultures are serial passages of a few lines. The *P* values treat cultures as units; the number of cell lines behind each arm (two to seven; Supplementary Table 2a) and, for oxygen, the per-line differences in Supplementary Table 2d are the conservative reading.

# Supplementary Results 2. Which genes keep an age association

If division rate drives the association, individual genes should retain their age association in proportion to how loosely they are coupled to division. In the 107 adult donors, 137 of the 177 genes show an age association and every one of them is a decline. Once a transcriptional proliferation score is included, 19 keep a significant decline and none acquires one (Supplementary Fig. S2c). That last statement holds for the primary cohort only: two genes acquire an association after adjustment in the 133-donor definition and eight in the deposited 142-sample set (Supplementary Table 1). The genes are individually well coupled to proliferation (median *r* = 0.64; Supplementary Fig. S2b), and the share of the age effect a gene keeps falls as that coupling rises (ρ = −0.45, 95% CI −0.56 to −0.32, *P* = 3 × 10⁻¹⁰, n = 177 genes). A gene's retained share and its correlation with the covariate being adjusted for are not independent quantities, so this relationship is expected in part from the adjustment itself and is not an independent test. The 19 genes that keep a decline are somewhat less tightly coupled than the rest (median 0.49 against 0.65), although the difference is not significant (*P* = 0.16). None of them is one of the twenty factors the ageing literature assays: of the eleven of those factors that belong to the set, none keeps an age association once proliferation is included (Supplementary Fig. S2a).

The set is not carried by its non-spliceosomal members. Restricted to the 127 genes that belong to Reactome mRNA splicing itself, the age effect and its attenuation are the same as for the whole set (−0.132 per decade, 66% removed by adjustment), and so they are for the remaining 50 genes of capping, 3′-end processing and export (−0.136, 78% removed; Supplementary Table 3); scored in one matrix, the two subsets follow the measured division rate equally closely (ρ = 0.66 and 0.70, whole set 0.68 in that matrix; see Supplementary Results 6).

Those 19 genes include core spliceosomal and 3′-processing components — *SF3A3*, *SF3B6*, *SNRPF*, *PPIH*, *PPIL1*, *SNW1*, *CDC5L*, *CWC22*, *CWC27*, *MAGOH*, *PNN*, *HNRNPC*, *RBMX*, *PABPN1*, *ZCRB1*, *CCAR1*, *IK*, *THOC2* and *NUP88*. They are not, however, a stable set. The same analysis repeated under the other cohort definitions keeps 40 genes in the deposited 142-sample set, 56 when only progeria donors are removed, 19 in the primary cohort and none among adults aged 20 to 82 (Fig. 4d, Supplementary Table 1). No gene is therefore common to all four definitions; the three definitions that keep any gene share ten (*IK*, *MAGOH*, *PABPN1*, *PNN*, *PPIH*, *PPIL1*, *SF3A3*, *SF3B6*, *SNRPF* and *SNW1*). In the primary specification a decline remains after adjustment at the level of the whole set, but which genes carry it depends on how the cohort is drawn, and no subset of these genes is recommended here as an alternative index of ageing.

# Supplementary Results 3. The splicing outcome index carries no age association in the adult cohort

Splicing-factor abundance and the accuracy of splicing are different quantities, and the tissue evidence for age-associated splicing dysfunction rests on the latter^8^. The donor cohort was reprocessed uniformly with junction-level output^17^, so both could be taken from the same libraries and treated identically.

The outcome measure does not support an ageing interpretation in this cohort. The share of junction-spanning reads falling on junctions absent from the reference annotation is only weakly related to proliferation (*r* = −0.27, n = 107 donors), and in the 107 adult donors it has no association with age (β = +1.5 × 10⁻⁵ per decade, 95% CI −3.0 × 10⁻⁵ to +6.0 × 10⁻⁵, *P* = 0.51), before or after adjustment (Supplementary Fig. S3a, b). The same analysis run on all 142 deposited samples gives an age association (*P* = 0.002) that the cohort definition removes: progeria donors and children contribute to it, and the 31 donors aged 83 and over — every one of them from the same cell repository, which has a higher unannotated-junction fraction at every age — carry the remainder. Among adults aged 20 to 82 the estimate runs in the opposite direction (*P* = 0.012). The index also behaves like a measure of library quality rather than of biology: across these donors it falls with the proportion of uniquely mapped reads (ρ = −0.43) and rises with multi-mapping (ρ = +0.42) and intronic signal (ρ = +0.31), and adding those quality measures to the model removes what little age association is left (*P* = 0.96), while the age effect on splicing-factor expression survives them (−0.110 per decade, *P* = 7 × 10⁻⁶, and −0.039 after adjustment for proliferation, *P* = 0.003; Supplementary Table 3). The entropy of junction usage behaves the same way. This index was not specified in advance of the analyses in which it first appeared and is reported as exploratory.

Event-level splicing carries the same warning (Supplementary Fig. S3c, d). The number of age-associated events falls from 135, 302, 332 and 98 under the four cohort definitions to 20, 22, 14 and none once proliferation is included. The *COL1A2* event with the strongest age association in the deposited set is flat among adults below 83 (PSI 0.035 to 0.049 by age band; ρ = 0.12, *P* = 0.29, n = 76 adults aged 20 to 82; the ρ = 0.22 printed in Supplementary Fig. S3d includes donors under 20) and steps up to 0.072 in the 83-and-over stratum, and the distal junction that produces it is absent from GENCODE v41. Whether splicing outcome deteriorates with age in these cells therefore cannot be decided from this cohort, and the tissue-level evidence for age-associated splicing degeneration^8^ stands on its own measurements, not on these.

# Supplementary Results 4. A transcriptome-wide age predictor tracks proliferation (post hoc)

The same reading applies to age predictors built from these cells. A ridge model trained and cross-validated on all expressed genes in the 107 donors predicts chronological age with *r* = 0.72 (95% CI 0.61 to 0.80; mean absolute error 14 years), far outside a permuted-age null (20 permutations, *r* = −0.31 to 0.40; Supplementary Table 3). Its predictions track the proliferation score at *r* = −0.74, and proliferation accounts for 54% of the variance in predicted age (Supplementary Table 3), while the 20-gene proliferation score by itself predicts age at *r* = 0.46 (mean absolute error 18 years). A model built after removing the cohort covariates and proliferation from every gene gives *r* = −0.53, but its predictions are nearly constant (57.5 to 59.0 years) and its error equals that of assigning every donor the cohort's mean age (21.6 against 21.5 years), so it carries no information about age. That arm cannot be read as isolating proliferation either: removing the cohort's technical covariates alone already lowers accuracy to *r* = 0.24, and repository membership cannot be separated from proliferation in this collection.

# Supplementary Results 5. Reprogramming media and secretomes (post hoc class tests)

Interventions intended to rejuvenate fibroblasts almost all make them divide faster^18^, which makes them the sharpest test of the coupling. Seven reprogramming contrasts — naive and primed media, NHSM, RSeT and 5iLAF formulations^19^, OSK induction in fibroblasts from a 96-year-old donor and the matched arm in which wild-type OCT4 is replaced by the point mutant OCT4YR — and fourteen cell-free secretome preparations applied to recipient fibroblasts were examined on the same terms. One contrast previously counted as reprogramming was reclassified: in that series both the day-3 and the day-7 fibroblast samples are Sendai-OSKM-transduced cells still in fibroblast medium, the switch to reprogramming media having occurred on day 8, so their comparison measures four further days of culture.

Within each class the change in splicing-factor expression rises with the change in the cell cycle (reprogramming media ρ = 0.89, 95% CI 0.43 to 0.98, *P* = 0.007, n = 7 contrasts from two series; secretomes ρ = 0.63, 0.14 to 0.87, *P* = 0.017, n = 14 contrasts from eight series; Supplementary Fig. S4c). The within-class slope for reprogramming media is steeper than the slope outside the class (1.68, 95% CI 0.82 to 2.55, against 0.64) and depends on the naive-medium contrast (1.19, −0.64 to 3.02, without it). Predicted from a fit that excludes the class being predicted, 12 of the 21 interventions lie within 0.05 of the prediction and 7 lie more than 0.10 from it (Supplementary Fig. S4b): naive reprogramming medium (+0.34), NHSM (+0.12) and one serum-derived preparation (+0.14) above it, and OSK induction (−0.18), the OCT4YR arm (−0.14), a trophoblast stem cell secretome (−0.20) and a mesenchymal stromal cell secretome (−0.13) below it. In naive medium *PNISR*, *LSM14A*, *HNRNPD*, *SRSF7* and *HNRNPA1* rise by 0.91 to 1.44 in log₂ (Supplementary Fig. S4c). Across the seven reprogramming contrasts *PNISR*, *SRSF2* and *TRA2B* rise in all seven (mean log₂ 0.49 to 0.69), *HNRNPA1* and *HNRNPD* in five, *SRSF1* and *AKAP17A* barely move, and *IMP3* falls (mean −0.45).

Across all 21 intervention contrasts the median absolute residual is 0.045 against a median absolute change of 0.111 when each class is predicted from a fit that excludes it (0.038 from the in-sample fit of all 63 contrasts), so about three-fifths of a typical intervention effect is what its effect on the cell cycle predicts. The share differs by class: about half for secretomes (0.041 against 0.082) and about one-ninth for reprogramming media (0.120 against 0.135), whose individual conditions depart from the line in both directions while their class mean does not (+0.02, 95% CI −0.14 to +0.18). With seven contrasts from two series, five of them sharing one reference arm, that class test could not detect a shift smaller than about 0.22, which is larger than the class's typical effect. The secretome class mean is −0.02 (−0.07 to +0.02, n = 14 contrasts from eight series; detectable shift about 0.07). For secretomes about half of a typical change in splicing-factor transcripts is what the cell-cycle response predicts; for reprogramming media most of it is not, in either direction. A rise in splicing-factor transcripts after an intervention therefore cannot be interpreted without a division-rate measurement alongside the transcriptome.

# Supplementary Results 6. Which part of the expression machinery lies closest to division (post hoc)

Splicing is not the only part of the machinery of gene expression that follows the cell cycle, so the splicing set was compared with three Reactome sets scored the same way: Translation, Metabolism of RNA and rRNA processing. The 177-gene set was selected for declining in three datasets, one of which compares late with early passages in the very resource where division rate falls with passage. The comparison was therefore run with two unselected definitions as well — Reactome mRNA Splicing (215 genes) and Processing of Capped Intron-Containing Pre-mRNA (296 genes) — and with the pathway genes lying outside the 177 as a lower bound. No comparison shares a gene: shared genes were removed from the comparator for the selected set and from both sides for the other definitions (removing them from both sides for the selected set as well changes no partial correlation by more than 0.01), except that Metabolism of RNA contains both splicing pathways, so there the splicing genes were removed from the comparator only.

Against the counted division rate (328 libraries) every splicing definition follows the rate more closely than the comparators do (selected set ρ = 0.68, mRNA Splicing 0.63, pre-mRNA processing 0.65; comparators 0.43 to 0.52). The selected set reaches 0.71 with the score of Fig. 2a; here every set is scored in the expression matrix of the programme audit so that all sets share one filter, and 176 of the 177 genes pass it. Holding a comparator constant leaves the splicing definition with most of its coupling (partial ρ = 0.56 to 0.59 for the selected set, 0.43 to 0.51 for mRNA Splicing, 0.52 to 0.55 for pre-mRNA processing), while holding the splicing definition constant leaves the comparators with none (−0.22 to +0.01; Supplementary Fig. S5a). With the unselected definitions the asymmetry is 66 to 91% of its size with the selected set. It is carried by the selected genes: for the 119 pre-mRNA processing genes outside the 177 it reverses (splicing partial −0.07 to +0.15, comparators 0.30 to 0.50).

In the 107 donors the same holds for the age effect. Selection inflated only the unadjusted effect (−0.133 per decade for the selected set against −0.094 for mRNA Splicing); the proliferation-adjusted residue is the same (−0.040 and −0.042), and with translation also in the model it is −0.022 (*P* = 0.044) and −0.023 (*P* = 0.015), whereas the decline of translation and of Metabolism of RNA disappears once either splicing score is in the model (−0.001 to +0.009, *P* = 0.17 to 0.94; Supplementary Fig. S5c).

GTEx cultures do not reproduce the asymmetry. There proliferation is the 20-marker transcriptional score, not a counted rate, and against it translation lies closer than splicing does: holding the other constant, translation keeps 0.51 to 0.59 and the splicing definitions 0.28 to 0.41 (n = 511 cultures; Supplementary Fig. S5b). Against Metabolism of RNA and against rRNA processing the selected set keeps an asymmetry (0.60 against −0.29, and 0.53 against 0.26) but the unselected definitions do not (differences within their bootstrap intervals). The asymmetry therefore appears where division was counted and in the donor cohort, and not against a transcriptional proliferation score in an independent collection. These scores are strongly correlated with one another (*r* = 0.68 to 0.89 in the counted libraries), so what the comparison can establish is which of them lies closest to division rate, not that they are independently regulated programmes (Supplementary Table 5).

# Supplementary Results 7. Seven senescence panels (post hoc)

Because the choice of panel decides the answer, five further panels were scored the same way as the Reactome set: SenMayo^22^, the up and down halves of the fibroblast senescence signature of Fridman and Tainsky^36^, and the CellAge genes annotated as inducing or as inhibiting senescence^37^, with the Reactome mitotic cell cycle as the positive control. Across the seven, panels with a larger share of cell-cycle genes tend to correlate more strongly with the measured division rate (ρ = 0.71, exact two-sided *P* = 0.088, n = 7 panels; Supplementary Fig. S6a); with seven panels the trend does not reach significance. Reactome Cellular Senescence (55% cell-cycle genes) reaches ρ = +0.67 and falls to +0.38 when those genes are removed; SenMayo (1%) −0.10; Fridman up (15%) −0.07; Fridman down (27%, eleven genes measured) +0.38, falling to +0.01; the CellAge inducers (10%) +0.33. The CellAge genes annotated as inhibiting senescence do not fit the composition account: 21% of them are cell-cycle genes, yet the panel reaches +0.69 and keeps +0.71 after those genes are removed, as might be expected of genes whose activity lets cells keep dividing.

In the 107 donors, five of the seven panels have an age association before adjustment (FDR < 0.05): Reactome Cellular Senescence (−0.026 per decade), the CellAge inhibitors (−0.041), Fridman down (−0.034) and the cell-cycle control (−0.088) decline, and Fridman up rises (+0.043). SenMayo and the CellAge inducers have none (FDR = 0.72). None of the five keeps its association once proliferation is included; they lose 66 to 100% of the estimate (Supplementary Fig. S6b, Supplementary Table 7).

# Supplementary Results 8. A donor's cultured fibroblasts do not track that donor's skin

Programme-level state is not shared between a donor's cultured fibroblasts and that donor's skin. Among 388 donors who contributed both cultured fibroblasts and skin, no programme score in the cultured cells was related to the same programme in that donor's skin (ρ = −0.10 to 0.13, none with FDR < 0.05; Supplementary Fig. S8c); this concordance was exploratory analysis S3 of the preregistration, for which no success criterion was set (Supplementary Note 1). That is not a failure of the measurement: in the same donors and the same matrices, genes whose expression is strongly genotype-dependent are recovered across the two sample types (*GSTM1* ρ = 0.75, *ERAP2* ρ = 0.75, both *P* < 10⁻⁷⁰; seven sex-chromosome genes 0.67 to 0.74); this positive control was added post hoc. Programme scores measured in cultured fibroblasts therefore carry no detectable information about the same programmes in the donor's bulk skin (|ρ| ≤ 0.13, n = 388 donors); because bulk skin is mostly not fibroblasts, this comparison cannot separate the effect of culture from that of cell type.
